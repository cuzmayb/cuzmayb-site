## Three principles for decisions

1. **Avoid disqualification, above all.** I memorized the disqualification conditions in the rules first.
2. **The model, the documents, and the numbers have to agree.**
3. **Points ride on what the judges see at a glance.**

## The questions kept moving outward

Chip → module → "where does the water go" → tray → rack → "how does it look on the judges' screen". Each time, one short question opened the next level.

## Turning unsupported numbers into supported ones

The loads I first put into Generative Design were assumed values, and the safety factors came out at 8~277. That means minimum thickness, not stress, determined the shape. I discarded the first results and took loads only from datasheets and standards.

Looking at the results, the consolidated part was 4.4 times heavier than before. Writing "lightweighting" would have been false, so I changed the story to "consolidated 4 parts into 1."

## Listing facts hides what is new

The biggest deduction in my self-scoring was that the model was the latest version while the description document was stuck on old numbers. Rewriting the document from scratch, I switched from "list every fact" to "claim first, one supporting number."

## What remains

The write-up will not score any higher. The remaining points come from analysis (CFD) and a physical build.
