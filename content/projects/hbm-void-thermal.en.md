> The data on this page is synthetic, generated by simulation. There is no measurement of a stacked die here.

## Problem

HBM stacks 12 DRAM dies with bonding layers in between. A hand calculation showed that the bonding-layer thermal conductivity alone moves the temperature difference across the stack from 0.5 K to 137 K. A void in a bonding layer blocks heat. I wanted to know whether voids can be found without cutting the chip, using only the curve of temperature rise after power-on, Zth(t).

1. From what void size does detection start?
2. Can it tell which layer the void is in?
3. What sets that limit?

## Decisions and why

### Method

| Step | Description |
|---|---|
| Model | 1D RC ladder of the 12-high stack, 10 segments per layer. Area 112.4 mm², bonding layer 15 µm thick, conductivity 0.5 W/m·K. Matched to the analytical solution within 1 % and to the hand-calculation table with a 2 % tolerance |
| Forward | 1 W step at the junction → Zth(t) |
| Inverse | Zth → time-constant spectrum → Cauer network → structure function |
| Defect | In one bonding layer, conduction is cut over a fraction of the area. This approximates small scattered voids |
| Detection | Defective if it exceeds the 99th percentile of 300 good units |
| Location | Fit the difference between the defective unit and the good-unit mean to templates of 12 layers × void size |
| External check | Ran the conditions and measured curves from IBM literature (Smith 2007) through the same pipeline |

### Changed the pass/fail rule from 3σ to the 99th percentile

At first a unit was called defective when it was more than 3σ above the good-unit mean. An independent review, done by reading only the files with no context, pointed out that the real false-alarm rate of that rule was 2.15 %. The distribution of total thermal resistance among good units was skewed. I changed the rule to the 99th percentile of 300 good units and measured the false-alarm rate again on 2000 new good units. At noise of 0.01, 0.1 and 1 % it was 1.25, 0.95 and 3.0 %.

### Replaced the bonding-layer input with measured ranges and ran it again

The first bonding-layer input, an area resistance of 30 mm²·K/W, was calculated from literature properties of an adhesive film (NCF). Production processes and measurement papers gave 4.2 to 19 mm²·K/W for µbump with underfill and 0.80 to 1.28 mm²·K/W for hybrid bonding. The input was 1.6 to 7 times larger than the measurements. I changed only the bonding-layer value and ran the same pipeline again. The result is the table under "By bonding process" below.

### Found why the 2D templates did worse than the 1D ones

A void in one lump cannot be solved in 1D, so I built a separate 2D axisymmetric model. Fitting 1D templates to data made by the 2D model gave 48 % accuracy within ±1 layer at layers 6 and 9 of the µbump stack. I then built 2D templates, and at first they did worse than 1D. There were two causes. The size grid was coarse, so a template with the wrong shape won because its amplitude happened to fit. The good-unit reference curve came from a different mesh, and that difference (0.02 to 0.08 %) was as large as the void signal. After fixing both, the same layers reached 98 % or higher.

### Narrowed the scope to the test-chip stage

The on-die temperature sensor in the HBM standard (JESD235D) reports in 1 °C steps. The noise this method assumes, 0.1 %, is about 3.7 mK at 1 W of heating. The built-in sensor cannot deliver that. So I limited the scope to process development with thermal test chips. It does not fit field diagnosis of production parts.

## Results

### With no spread

With measurement noise at or below 0.1 % of the final Zth, voids from 2.5 % area are detected over 90 % of the time in all 12 layers. The layer is located within ±1 over 90 % of the time in all 12 layers from 10.5 % area. At 5.5 % it is 75 to 100 % depending on the layer. With 1 % noise, detection starts at 20.5 % and location at 50.5 %.

![Detection rate and location accuracy versus void area](/images/hbm-void-detection.webp "Detection rate (left) and location accuracy within ±1 layer (right) versus void area. Worst of the 12 layers, with no spread among good units. Synthetic data, drawn from the thermal-stack result file w3_void.csv.")

### With spread among good units

If the bonding conductivity of each layer varies independently by j, the void area φ needed for 90 % detection grows as φ/(1−φ) = (3 + 1.28)·j·√12. At these areas the Monte Carlo detection rate was 82 to 100 %.

| Per-layer spread j | Area needed for 90 % detection | Top layer (12) located within ±1 |
|---|---|---|
| 0 | 2.5 % | Over 90 % from 10.5 % |
| 1 % | 12.9 % | 92 % at 25.5 % |
| 2 % | 22.9 % | 90 % at 40.5 % |
| 5 % | 42.6 % | Only 78 % even at 50.5 % |

![Detection rate versus void area for several levels of per-layer spread](/images/hbm-void-spread.webp "Detection rate with per-layer bonding conductivity spread of 0, 1, 2 and 5 %. Noise 0.1 %, worst of layers 1, 3, 6, 9 and 12. Synthetic data, drawn from w3_void.csv and w3_robust.csv.")

A spread of just 1 % moves the detection limit from 2.5 % to about 13 %. In that case the spread among good units sets the limit. This holds only when measurement noise is at or below 0.1 %, the temperature-sensitivity (K-factor) calibration error is within 0.1 %, and cold-plate repeatability is within 1 %. At 1 % noise, the resistance scatter from noise (0.012 K/W) is larger than the scatter from a 1 % spread (0.009 K/W). The calibration and remounting errors were only converted by calculation and were not simulated.

### Structure function and harm by layer

- The structure function shows only the boundary of the first bonding layer. Seen from the junction, a single pole with a time constant of 0.19 s carries 3.16 K/W of the total 3.73 K/W (85 %), and one bonding layer is 7 %. This is a limit of estimating the spectrum from a sampled curve. With exact input all 12 layers are recovered.
- Higher layers carry more heat, so the same void does more harm there. A 50 % void raises the junction temperature by 1.60 K in layer 1 and by 7.47 K in layer 12 (30 W operation). The layer the structure function shows is layer 1, the least harmful one, and with spread the upper layers lose their location first.

### By bonding process

Results with no spread and 0.1 % noise, changing only the bonding-layer area resistance.

| Bonding layer | Area resistance (mm²·K/W) | Area for 90 % detection | Junction rise from that void (top layer) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Uniform NCF assumption (first input) | 30 | 2.5 % | +0.19 K |
| µbump + underfill, measured | 19 to 8.0 | 2.5 % | +0.12 to 0.05 K |
| µbump + underfill, 40 µm pitch | 4.2 | 5.5 % | +0.06 K |
| Hybrid bonding | 1.28 to 0.80 | 5.5 to 10.5 % | +0.02 K |

- As the bonding layer improves, scattered voids become harder to see, but they become harmless faster than that.
- A void in one lump behaves differently. A circular void of 3 % area at the center of the top layer, solved in 2D, raises the peak junction temperature by 0.85 to 1.23 K. The same area scattered gives 0.006 to 0.23 K.
- Feeding data made by the 2D model into the same algorithm, a single lump void of 1 % area was still detected 95 to 100 % of the time (no spread).

### One external measurement

I read the three measured transient curves in Fig. 2 of Smith 2007 (IBM), with TIM thicknesses of 4.2, 23.7 and 34.2 µm, off the figure and ran them through the same pipeline. From the difference between the 23.7 µm and 4.2 µm curves, the added resistance was read as 0.0387 K·cm²/W in the TIM layer. The true value is 0.0406, an error of −5 %. The two pairs that include the 34.2 µm curve were read 15 % and 33 % low.

## Limits and next steps

- The data is synthetic. The only measurement is one set of literature curves from a single chip with TIM. Nothing here was measured on a stacked die.
- The 1D model assumes heat spreads fully sideways around a void. That is optimistic for a large single void, so I solved that case separately in 2D axisymmetric form. Off-center voids and multiple voids need 3D.
- The heating point and the sensing point are assumed to be the same. The positions and resolution of a real heater and temperature sensor are not in the model.
- The bonding-layer area resistances are measurements on test stacks. There are no values for HBM products. The heat capacity of NCF has no source, so the underfill value was used in its place.
- I could not find the real size of the spread among good units, or an allowed temperature, in the literature. So I made no pass/fail call and left them as variables.
- Next: add TIM and cold-plate terms to the templates and check whether remounting scatter and a top-layer void separate by time constant. The program already takes a measured curve as two columns, time and Zth, once one is available.
